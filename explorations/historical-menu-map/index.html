A city of appetites

More than one
Philly sandwich.

Steak sandwiches, roast pork, oyster houses, banquet rooms. There is more than one way to read a city's food history.

Steak-sandwich origins are usually credited to the Olivieri family in the 1930s; the date cheese joined the sandwich is less certain. Roast pork belongs to a wider Italian American tradition, rather than one proven inventor.

Read the Inquirer's history and Visit Philadelphia's sandwich guide. These are context, not extra menu records.

How to read this atlas

An archive has edges.

These library collections are a starting point. They do not represent complete or balanced coverage of Philadelphia. Records from different libraries have not been deduplicated.

Undated menus remain undated. Unresolved venues remain in the list. Pins represent reviewed locator points, not historical building outlines. Some catalog dates have more precision than the printed menu supports; each record explains its dating basis.

Prices are transcribed historical amounts. Missing prices stay missing; there is no inflation conversion without a verified CPI series. Scans open at the holding library, where image rights can be checked.
